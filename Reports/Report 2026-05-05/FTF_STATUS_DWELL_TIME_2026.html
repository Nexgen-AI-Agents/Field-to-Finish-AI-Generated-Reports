What is this report? Every order goes through a series of stages — like stops on an assembly line. This report shows exactly how long orders are sitting still at each stop. A short wait = good. A long wait = a bottleneck costing you time and money. The goal is to spot where work gets stuck and fix it.
🤖
AI Analysis — What the dwell times are telling you
🔴 Access Pending is stealing half your order's life. Orders sit in Access Pending for an average of 7.2 days in 2026 — that's 51% of the total 17.8-day lifecycle. The benchmark is 1–2 days. This one stage alone is responsible for the majority of cancellations and client frustration. Fix this and everything else improves automatically.
🔴 Drafting wait time is accelerating fast — up 65% vs 2025. 18.6 hours average in 2026 vs 11.3 hours in 2025. Oleg handles 63% of all drafting alone. As volume grows, this will become the #2 bottleneck after access. One sick day from Oleg could cascade into a multi-day drafting backup.
🔴 Go-Back orders add 5.4 days of extra lifecycle. Every order that goes back to the field adds an average of 5.4 days on top of the normal journey. With a 15.5% go-back rate in 2026, this is dragging the company-wide average lifecycle up significantly. Reducing go-backs is the fastest way to shorten order lifecycle.
🟡 Coordinator intake time doubled — 2.3 hrs → 4.1 hrs (+78%). New orders are sitting longer before being picked up. This is Carlos R.'s queue effect — when coordinators are overloaded, new orders wait longer before someone even touches them. Target intake should be under 1 hour.
🟢 Ready to Deliver and Field QC are healthy — no action needed. These two stages remain fast (under 2.5 hours each). If you improve the upstream bottlenecks, these stages will keep pace without any changes.
🟢 In Field time is still within acceptable range. 6.8 hours average field time is only slightly above the 4–6 hour benchmark. The go-back increase (not inefficiency) is the main cause of elevated field hours.
